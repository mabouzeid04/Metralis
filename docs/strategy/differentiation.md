# 3. What We Are NOT

## 3.1 We Are Not a CMMS  
A CMMS is a database with CRUD screens: log issues, assign tickets, close them.

Problems with CMMS systems:

- terrible UX  
- technician adoption is low  
- logs are incomplete  
- data is inconsistent and unusable  
- no semantic understanding  
- no leverage from historical patterns  
- documentation lives in PDFs, not in workflows  

Traditional CMMS =  
**record-keeping tool**  
Our product =  
**intelligence engine**

We do include CMMS-like functionality because we must capture data —  
but CMMS is the *shell*, not the *purpose*.

## 3.2 We Are Not an IoT Platform  
IoT platforms:

- focus on sensors  
- require installations  
- collect raw time-series data  
- rely on heavy engineering effort  
- often fail due to lack of context  

They can tell you:  
“Temperature is rising.”  
They cannot tell you:  
“Why, based on 7 similar failures across 4 other machines?”

We don’t sell hardware.  
We don’t require retrofits.  
We don’t depend on vibration-analysis algorithms.

We focus on **context, not signals**.

## 3.3 We Are Not Predictive Maintenance  
Predictive maintenance players chase:

- anomaly detection  
- time-to-failure estimates  
- threshold alarms  

These solutions struggle because:

- they require ideal sensor data  
- they rarely understand the *meaning* of the anomaly  
- they cannot reason over symptoms, manuals, or past fixes  
- they cannot incorporate technician input  

Predictive maintenance =  
**pattern recognition**  
We are building =  
**causal understanding**

We may use predictive insights in the future, but the core is reasoning, not signals.

# 5. Why Technician-Centric Workflows Matter

Technicians control the majority of factory knowledge:

- symptoms they observe  
- steps they take  
- tricks and hacks that work  
- lessons they learn  
- the true sequence of events  

Technicians are the **primary contributors** to machine memory.

Most industrial software:

- ignores technician experience  
- is slow and painful to use  
- treats technicians like data-entry clerks  
- fails because adoption fails  

We invert this:

- conversational AI-first workflows  
- instant logging by voice or natural language  
- AI extracting structure from messy technician input  
- AI assisting during diagnosis, not after  

By building for technicians first:

- adoption rises  
- data quality improves  
- the system’s intelligence compounds  

This is the only viable path to a usable digital twin.
